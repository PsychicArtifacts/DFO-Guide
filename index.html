The Hidden Ladders

Every sign in the Bay is a rung.

Daggerfall never tells you a shop is quality nineteen. It burns incense and plays soft music. It never says a dungeon ranks twenty-one. It calls the place a Coven and lets you work out the rest.

The numbers are real and they run under everything. This key teaches the signs first and keeps the ladder underneath. Set your sigil and every reading below bends to your Luck, your level and your Renown. When you want the bare numbers, lift the veil.

Ladders are cut in ogham, the tree alphabet. Trade climbs the old metals: ♄︎ lead to ☉︎ gold.

The Counter

A shop tells you its rank in the first thing it says. Five rungs, lead to gold. Buy in lead. Sell in gold.

What did the shop say when you walked in?

  • Shelves count your Luck in whole tens. At 69 you shop like 60. One more point is a whole step.
  • Only pawn shops conjure magic items, and only from the middle of the copper rung up.
  • Gem stores need the silver rung for emeralds, and one step more for diamonds and sapphires.
  • Alchemists hide their rare reagents in fine rooms. Mummy Wrappings, Gryphon Feather and Nymph Hair need the silver rung, Holy Relic needs incense, and Dragon’s Scales only the very finest incense. Unicorn Horn, Basilisk Eye and Pearl sit on every counter.
  • A finer shop watches its shelves. Its rank adds straight to your chance of being caught stealing.

The Descent

The travel map prints a dungeon’s kind, and the kind is its rank. It decides who lives there, how good the metal runs, and how often the dead carry magic. Two ladders, one name.

What are you going down for?

What the dead carry, at your Luck

  • Metal and magic climb different ladders. A Giant Stronghold drops thin metal but richer magic than a Prison.
  • Human Strongholds, Prisons and Ruined Castles rank by your character level, up to a Vampire Haunt’s metal.
  • Mines, caves and nests sit at the bottom of the metal ladder however hard they fight.
  • The gold run: Desecrated Temples hold the most daedra. Covens hold the best metal and four daedra besides. Bring mithril.

The Forge

Ten metals, iron to daedric. Some foes refuse a lesser metal. Online the blow still lands, a fifth softer for every rung you fall short, and never under a tenth.

What is your blade made of?

  • Silver is steel’s twin everywhere else and full strength on every ghost, wraith and vampire. Carry one early.
  • Fists and spells ignore metal entirely. The grove was never fussy about ore.
  • Silver and adamantium hold more enchantment than mithril.
  • Leather is 3 armour and chain 6. Shields ignore metal: buckler 1, round 2, kite 3, tower 4.

The Harvest

Seven rungs of rarity, and only the first four ever fall from the dead. What you kill and where you kill it set the odds. Luck only leans on them.

The last three never roll: Aetheric pieces are a boss’s own set, Artifacts are Daggerfall’s, and Gilded is one item, the Hourlock.

What did you just bring down?

  • Bad-luck protection is real. Every 25 pieces you take below Legendary raise the Legendary odds by a fifth, up to triple. Taking one resets it.
  • Rare pieces drop unidentified, and their lines already work before you know them.
  • Salvage breaks a piece into Welkynd Shards: Magic 1, Rare 3, Legendary 8, Exalted 15. The Hone’s price doubles with every hone.
  • Rolled resistances stop at 45% an element. Race and spells stack on top.

The Doors

Dair is the oak and the door both. Every guild has ten ranks and the same four keys at every door.

Which door are you knocking on?

5
  • The fastest road to Daedra summoning is the Mages Guild or Julianos, both at rank 6.
  • A knight’s Seneschal offers plate in the rank’s own metal, and you keep one piece. Rank 5 is mithril, the cheapest in the Bay.
  • Stendarr grants a rank-in-50 chance to survive a killing blow. At rank 9 that’s nearly one death in five, shrugged off.
  • The Thieves Guild never expels you. Fighters earn 10% more on quests and pay 10% less for repairs with every rank.

The Kindling

You carry two levels. Character level comes from your skills and sets what spawns, how hard your spells hit and how thin corpse gold runs. Renown is online’s own, kept on the server, and adds 3 health and 2 magicka a level.

What the tables send at you

Your Renown

Party in the room

Regular

Foes as Daggerfall made them. One dungeon in five holds a single elite. Comes back in 20 real minutes.

Elite

×2 health, ×2 damage. Three or four elites at ×7 health and ×4 damage. Comes back in 40 minutes.

Abyss

Foes level 24 or more at ×4 health and ×2.5 damage, elites ×9 and ×4.5. Rises 2 to 4 squares from the hub city of the busiest region, stands 48 real hours unbeaten, and collapses 3 minutes after it falls. The next rises 2 hours later.

Spoils for every fighter: 400 gold a level, a piece that is Legendary one time in four (else Rare), two more Rare or better, a Brass of Numidium piece one in three, and the Hourlock one in fifty.

  • A dungeon’s host rolls the loot by the host’s Renown. In a group, the highest Renown should host.
  • Class foes (mages, knights, thieves) stand at your character level, so they pay less Renown than a monster of the same fight.
  • An account earns at most 15,000 Renown an hour. Raids pay three quests; Sethrakul pays six.
  • Open-world elites are one foe in twenty from level 3: two Magic pieces, a 6% Legendary and 20 to 60 gold a level.

The Wheel

Online, a game day lasts two real hours and the year turns every thirty real days. The money holidays land everywhere at once. Times below are in your own time zone.

  • A festival half doesn’t stack with online’s half-price potions, and a shop still pays you at most half its full price. Festival buys can’t be flipped.
  • Online the summoner calls whichever prince you name, on any day, for 200,000 gold less 1,000 for every point of regard. The Glenmoril witches always call Hircine.
